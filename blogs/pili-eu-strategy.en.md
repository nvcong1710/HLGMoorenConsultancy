# From “Novel Food” Approval to EU Market Access: The European Entry Strategy for the Philippine Pili Industry

Obtaining EU approval as a “novel food” does not automatically translate into sales success in Europe. For many food businesses, it is merely the entry ticket. The real challenge begins afterward: understanding EU regulatory systems, distribution structures, consumer behavior, and country-specific positioning strategies.

The “Food Philippines Outbound Business Mission,” led by the Philippine Department of Trade and Industry (DTI) in the Netherlands and Belgium, was organized in this context—with a clear objective: **to establish a sustainable market entry foundation for pili products in Europe.**

## Strategic Approach

During business roundtables at the Philippine Embassies in The Hague and Brussels, the approach was structured around three pillars:

### 1️⃣ Regulatory Intelligence – Understand the System Before Selling

In the Netherlands, senior food expert Hugo Mooren provided guidance on:

- Navigating harmonized EU food regulations
- Distinguishing between EU-level requirements and country-specific regulations
- The importance of regulatory compliance prior to commercial negotiations

In Brussels, EU policy experts elaborated on:

- The Novel Food authorization mechanism
- Legal responsibilities of EU importers
- The importer’s role within the European value chain

Core message:  
**Regulatory compliance is a prerequisite for building trust with European importers.**

---

### 2️⃣ Market Entry Strategy – The Netherlands as a Gateway

The Netherlands is the largest nut importer in the EU, largely due to the Port of Rotterdam. Therefore, the recommended strategy included:

- Positioning the Netherlands as the “gateway market”
- Partnering with specialized nut importers and sector-specific distributors
- Optimizing logistics and laboratory testing via local partners

The delegation engaged with:

- Logistics and cargo handling operators
- Accredited laboratories for nut testing

This provided exporters with practical insight into EU requirements such as aflatoxin control, moisture thresholds, and testing protocols.

---

### 3️⃣ Retail & Consumer Insight – Understanding the End Buyer

In Brussels, the delegation conducted on-site visits to:

- Luxury supermarkets
- Specialty stores
- HORECA channels
- Organic & vegan shops

Key insights:

- The EU market is not homogeneous
- Organic, vegan, and sustainability positioning are strong growth drivers
- Origin storytelling and social impact narratives add premium value

Brussels, as a diverse international hub, allowed exporters to evaluate consumer responses to ethnic premium products such as pili.

## Strategic Directions for the Pili Industry

From this mission, several long-term strategic directions were defined:

### 🔹 1. Product Positioning

- Premium, healthy, sustainable
- Origin-based storytelling (Bicol Region)
- Emphasis on community impact & ESG

---

### 🔹 2. Quality Standardization & Technical Dossiers

- Complete novel food compliance documentation
- Optimize labeling according to country-specific requirements
- Prepare comprehensive technical dossiers for importers

---

### 🔹 3. Channel Selection

- Specialized nut importers in the Netherlands
- Niche distributors for organic/vegan segments
- Premium HORECA channels for value-added products

---

### 🔹 4. Long-Term Strategy

- Build long-term relationships with EU importers
- Conduct pilot batch testing
- Gradually scale volumes rather than pushing large quantities immediately

The EU market prioritizes stability and consistency over rapid expansion.
